Working with the injected half of ArgMOD

I’ve ported most of ArgMOD to run through Refinish Metal’s module engine. This explains what changed, what you can now do from JSON, and where the edges are.

You don’t need to know Lua. Everything here happens in two files:

data/argmod_materials.json
data/argmod_reactions.json

What Refinish Metal is, and what “injected” means

Refinish Metal is a DFHack mod. The part that matters to you is its module engine: after a save loads, it writes materials and reactions directly into DF’s memory, the same C++ arrays the game filled from your raws a moment earlier. The game can’t tell the difference. An injected reaction shows up in the workshop menu and runs like any other.

Before every save it pulls all of that back out again, so nothing custom is ever written to the save file. What that definitely buys you: you can rewrite the injected half completely, change materials, add reactions, restructure the lot, and reload the same save with no migration and no regen. (Whether a save survives uninstalling the RM outright I’ve never tested; there’s still an info.txt registration in play.)

The payoff is that injected content is not part of worldgen. You can add, change or delete an injected material or reaction and it takes effect on the next launch, on an existing save, with no regen. Raws can’t do that. Change an [INORGANIC:] in raws and every existing world keeps the old one.

The cost is that this ArgMOD port requires Refinish Metal for the injected half to appear. Without it, the raw half loads normally and the rest simply isn’t there.

How much you can add

More than you’re used to budgeting for. Once content is injected it’s outside worldgen entirely, so the usual reason to be frugal with new inorganics doesn’t apply. Only inorganics added via raws will impact mineral diversity at worldgen, and once that issue is circumvented there is no further bottleneck which I’ve discovered beyond hardware limitations and menu bloat which can be managed with categories.

For scale: my own load order with every RM base metal active runs somewhere upwards of 100,000 materials and on the order of a million reactions and permissions. The only thing that grows with it is data-cycle latency: how long the engine takes to write everything in on load and pull it back out before a save. Worldgen is untouched. Data cycle latency and menu latency are the only limits left to manage.

Your injected half is 68 materials and 202 reactions. That’s a drop in the bucket once you escape worldgen. You could add a lot more stuff and it would still be a drop in the bucket. If you’re also running RM on top with all base metals selected and you have tens of thousands of refinished variations of all your base metals, you’ll have a lot of menu lag when clicking a magnifying glass which contains those inorganics. That being said, base metal selections are fully configurable in RM. You can even choose to leave all base metals unselected, at which point RM can operate as a module injection shell without ever building or loading its own refinishing assets at all.

The limit you will hit is the workshop menu: latency and clutter, because every reaction is a real entry in a real list. That’s what reaction categories are for. There may be better answers to menu clutter later; for now, categories.

The one rule that will break things

Raws are parsed when the world loads. Injection happens afterwards.

That gives references a direction:

Works?
Injected content → raw material Yes. Resolved at runtime, always fine.
Raw file → injected material No. The material doesn’t exist yet.

A raw file pointing at an injected material doesn’t error. It fails quietly, and the symptoms don’t look like a missing material:

  • an unresolved reagent material degrades to “any material of that item type”, so the reaction silently accepts the wrong ingredients
  • an unresolved product material leaves mat_index at −1, which the game renders as magma
  • an unresolved graphics binding drops the custom tile with no complaint

I hit all three during the port. If a reaction ever produces magma, this is why.

The practical consequence: if any raw file mentions a material, that material has to stay in raws. That’s why some of your metals are still in inorganic_metals_arg.txt despite being perfectly good injection candidates. SLAG and WROUGHT_IRON are named by raw reactions which can’t be injected; the ferroalloys are named by graphics_arg_ores.txt.

Where the definition now lives

For anything injected, the JSON is the definition. I deleted those [REACTION:...] and [INORGANIC:...] blocks from your raw files, so editing the raws for that content does nothing; there’s nothing left there to edit.

For anything still in raws, the raws remain the definition. The JSON can still reference those materials freely. That’s the whole point of the direction rule above, and injected reactions do it constantly (SLAG, WROUGHT_IRON and every vanilla metal are named from JSON). What the JSON doesn’t do is redefine them.

Nothing is defined in both places. If you’re unsure which half something lives in, search the JSON for its id first.

Adding a material

Each entry in argmod_materials.json is one [INORGANIC:...]. The key is the id without the ARGMOD_ prefix. The engine adds that, so DAMASCUS_STEEL becomes ARGMOD_DAMASCUS_STEEL in game.

The prefix isn’t cosmetic. It’s the only way the engine can tell its own objects apart from the game’s. Everything injected has to be found again and removed before every save, restored after, and matched back up to items in your fort across those cycles. That all keys off the prefix. Don’t set it to something another module might use, and don’t write pre-prefixed ids into key or you’d get ARGMOD_ARGMOD_X and the engine would lose track of it.

Colour priority, and Caldfir’s Metallic

color and fallback_colors are a priority chain. The engine tries color first, then each entry in fallback_colors in order, and takes the first one that resolves against the colour descriptors loaded in the game.

That gives you soft Metallic support with no detection and no configuration. Name the Metallic colour first and your own as the fallback:

"color": "METALLIC_STEEL",
"fallback_colors": ["EMENRIL","BLUE-GRAY"]

With Metallic loaded, the material comes out METALLIC_STEEL. Without it, that name doesn’t resolve, the chain falls through, and it comes out EMENRIL. If that fails too for some reason, it next becomes BLUE-GRAY. The same JSON works either way, and a player who adds or removes Metallic later just gets the next colour in priority on next load. You can chain as many as you like.

End the chain with something that definitely exists. If nothing in it resolves you get an arbitrary default rather than an error, which is a frustrating thing to debug. A vanilla colour as the last entry costs nothing.

This works with Metallic, and I checked. Its descriptor_color_1metallic.txt and descriptor_color_2divine.txt are [OBJECT:DESCRIPTOR_COLOR] files, so all 52 METALLIC_* names are real colour descriptors and the chain resolves them exactly like your own palette. No caveat.

Nothing was pre-loaded, because nothing could be. All 16 of your METALLIC_* recommendations sit on minerals or WROUGHT_IRON, and every one of them stayed in raws, and the fallback chain only exists for injected materials. Those stay manual [STATE_COLOR:...] edits, the way you have them now.

None of your 29 injected alloys currently uses a Metallic colour, so the chain is available and completely unused. If you’d like DAMASCUS_STEEL to match Metallic’s steel when it’s loaded, that’s "color": "METALLIC_STEEL" with "fallback_colors": ["DAMASCUS"] and nothing else.

One live collision. You and Metallic both recolour PIG_IRON: DARKSTEEL against METALLIC_PIG_IRON. Both are raw SELECT_INORGANIC edits, so whichever loads last wins, and neither of you can do anything about it from within your own mod. It’s the only overlap; Metallic recolours 32 vanilla materials and you touch 7, and PIG_IRON is the single intersection.

This is the clearest argument for the editing engine. Your Metallic recommendations are manual comments precisely because those materials are raw edits, and the engine can only add. Once it can edit existing materials, those SELECT_INORGANIC recolours could become injected edits carrying a fallback chain, and Metallic compatibility would just happen, on both sides, with no comments to action and no load-order coin flip on PIG_IRON.

Two places DF habits will mislead you

material_class is not USE_MATERIAL_TEMPLATE. A template gives you a set of defaults you then override. A class here applies a preset, and METAL turns on the entire capability set: weapons, armour, ammo, anvil, all of it.

Your metals aren’t uniform. Of the 46 you wrote, only 22 grant weapons and 9 grant anvil, and the ferroalloys grant nothing at all. So every material lists every flag explicitly, true or false. Leaving one out doesn’t withhold it the way omitting a token would: the preset grants it. Add a new ferroalloy without the flag block and it’ll come out forgeable into armour.

There’s no inheritance. Whatever a template would have given you (spec heat, densities, the whole strength block) has to be written out. The engine clones a donor material to get a valid structure, then blanks everything meaningful. If you leave a field out you get that donor value, either a wiped value or worse yet a cloned value which survived the wipe. The effect of this would depend on what object RM evaluated to be the best match on load, not on a value derived from any meaningful template.

  "skill": "MILLING",
  "fuel": false,
  "category": "ARGMOD_CAT_MILL_MINERAL_REAGENT",

    { "code": "bag", "type": "BAG",     "quantity": 1,

  ],

code is the reagent name you’d write in raws, and contains, to_container and get_material_product refer back to it. Codes must be unique within a reaction. DF tolerates duplicates when nothing references them; the RM engine doesn’t, because it resolves those links by name.

mat_id takes a full id: ARGMOD_ prefix for injected materials, bare id for vanilla or raw ones. ARGMOD_POTASSIUM_NITRATE but plain SALTPETER.

Reagent flags

reaction_class, has_material_reaction_product and metal_id sit directly on the reagent. Everything else goes in a flags object:

preserve, empty, does_not_determine_product_amount, in_container, unrotten, glass_material, sand_bearing, body_part, bone, shell, horn, fire_safe, magma_safe, non_economic, building_material, allow_melt_dump.

One has no raw equivalent: in_container. DF sets this for you when it sees another reagent’s [CONTAINS:] during parsing. Nothing parses the JSON that way, so you write it yourself: every powder that lives in a bag needs "in_container": true, and the bag needs "contains": "<that code>". Miss it and the reaction won’t find its reagent.

Permissions and fortress_mode

These are two separate switches that both sound like “can this be used”. Setting one doesn’t change the other.

reaction_flags.fortress_mode is a flag on the reaction itself, the direct equivalent of [FORTRESS_MODE_ENABLED]. It’s what makes the reaction usable in fortress mode at all. Leave it true on anything you want players to use. All 201 of your reactions that had the token still have it.

"reaction_flags": { "fortress_mode": true }

permissions replaces [PERMITTED_REACTION:...] in the entity file. It controls which civilisations the engine grants the reaction to. Four modes:

mode What the engine does
"ENTITY_CODE" Grants it to the entity raws you list. Needs an entities array. This is the direct translation of your entity file.
"NONE" Grants it to nobody.
"ALL" Grants it to every civilisation.
"AUTO" Decides per civilisation from their capabilities, e.g. only civs that can work metal get smelter reactions. 
"permissions": { "mode": "ENTITY_CODE", "entities": ["MOUNTAIN", "PLAINS", "EVIL"] }
"permissions": { "mode": "NONE" }

If you only care that it works in your fort: set fortress_mode true and you’re done. "mode": "NONE" is a perfectly valid setting and is what 74 of your reactions currently have, including the ore-weathering grinds and PURPLE_GOLD_MAKING, all of which work fine in a fort. A reaction with fortress_mode true and "mode": "NONE" is not misconfigured.

If you care what other civilisations do with it, that’s what permissions is for, and here the ground gets less certain, so I’d rather flag it than let you build on it:

  • Permits appear to apply to every civ of an entity type rather than just the player’s. [PERMITTED_REACTION:...] sits on the entity raw, and MOUNTAIN is one shared definition every mountain civilisation points at, so there’s no per-civ granularity to grant even in principle. I spent a long time on this and landed on “it must be all mountain civs”, but I never proved it.
  • Whether this remains true on fortress_mode alone and NPC civs then act on it (produce these items, bring them to trade) is something I haven’t yet confirmed firsthand.

Your content splits roughly in half here: 128 reactions carry entity permits and 74 don’t, following when things were written rather than any design line. Since fortress_mode covers your own fort either way, that inconsistency has probably never been visible in play.

Checking your work before you launch

preflight.py reads both JSON files and checks them against every rule the engine enforces, printing exactly what’s wrong and where. It reads the engine’s own type tables rather than a copy, so it can’t drift out of date as the engine gains types.

A pass means the module will load. It won’t catch design mistakes (a reaction nothing can reach, or a material nothing produces), but it catches every structural error, and it’s a second rather than a DF launch.

Running it

It’s a Python script, and Windows doesn’t ship with Python. If you already have it, skip to the file list.

Getting Python. Download it from python.org/downloads and run the installer. The only thing that matters is ticking “Add python.exe to PATH” on the first screen. It’s off by default and it’s the reason the command below wouldn’t otherwise work. Everything else can stay at its defaults. You don’t need to touch PowerShell’s execution policy; that applies to .ps1 scripts, not this.

Put four files in one folder, anywhere, it doesn’t have to be near the mod:

preflight.py
argmod_materials.json
argmod_reactions.json
refinish-module-types.lua

The last one comes from the Refinish Metal install and is how the script knows what the engine currently accepts. Take a fresh copy whenever RM updates, or you’ll get failures for reagent types that do actually exist. The script is only ever as current as that file.

Run it. In that folder, hold Shift and right-click on empty space → Open PowerShell window here. (Or click the folder’s address bar, type cmd, and press Enter.) Then:

python preflight.py

Reading the output. A pass looks like this:

engine tables read from refinish-module-types.lua: 24 reagent types, 10 product types, 12 buildings, 25 skills

PREFLIGHT PASSED: 68 materials, 202 reactions, 20 categories

A failure names the reaction, the reagent and the problem, so you can go straight to it:

PREFLIGHT FAILED: 1 problem(s):

  reactions[66] (PITCHBLENDE_REDUCTION).reagents[3] (caustic): type 'LIQUID_INORGANIC' requires mat_id

If it says python isn’t recognised as a command, the PATH box didn’t get ticked. Either rerun the installer and choose Modify, or try py preflight.py instead, which usually works regardless.

One thing to be aware of on the chemistry side

The engine picks a donor material and a template reaction for everything it injects by scoring candidates from the loaded game. That scoring was built around metals and dusts, which is what the mod originally did, and it shows: for your chemicals and chemist reactions the winning scores are low. The RAW-class donor scores a 3; the chemist reactions land on things like PRESS_OIL and ACACIA_BARK_DYE in the 50-70 range, against 85 for a typical smelter reaction.

Everything I’ve tested from that side behaves correctly, so low scores aren’t evidence of a problem unless you notice things aren’t behaving as intended. I don’t yet know whether the scoring is simply weak for this kind of content, or whether vanilla genuinely has nothing that’s a close structural match for a chemist reaction. If something in the chemistry behaves oddly in a way that isn’t a reagent problem, tell me. That’s the first place I’d look, and it’s my end to fix rather than yours.

What needs engine changes

These aren’t things you can do from JSON, so RM updates would be required before designing around them:

  • New reagent or product types. The tables above are the whole vocabulary. GLOB, plant and creature materials, tools other than jugs. Each needs a new entry.
  • New buildings. Vanilla ones are a table entry. A second custom workshop of yours works the same way the chemist does, but still needs that entry. Injecting buildings outright is probably possible; I just haven’t had reason to build it without encountering a building bottleneck.
  • Material properties not listed. absorption isn’t exposed, for instance.
  • Graphics. Bindings are parsed from raws and name materials directly, so an injected material can’t carry a custom tile. That’s the only reason the ferroalloys stayed behind.
  • Editing or removing existing content. The engine only adds. Anything you CUT or SELECT has to stay in raws. This is the big one; see the last section.

Bugs I found while porting

I ported every one of these exactly as written. The goal was a faithful port: the injected half behaves identically to your mod as it stands today, bugs included. I didn’t silently improve anything, because a port that quietly changes behaviour is worse than one that doesn’t, because you’d have no way to tell which differences were deliberate.

These are only things that appear to be mechanically broken. I’ve avoided treating things as bugs because there may be elements of design choice, but I wanted to point these ones out just in case you don’t know:

1. FERROTUNGSTEN_MAKING names both reagents A

DF tolerates it because nothing in that reaction refers to a code, but it’s a latent trap: the moment you add a CONTAINS or PRODUCT_TO_CONTAINER there it breaks. The RM engine requires unique codes, so during the port the second one became A2.

Fix in argmod_reactions.json: change that reagent’s "code": "A2" to "code": "B", or whatever element you choose.

2. SALT_GLAZE is referenced but not present

SODIUM_CHLORIDE and POTASSIUM_CHLORIDE both declare a GLAZE_MAT reaction product pointing at SALT_GLAZE, and it isn’t currently in the core mod. My guess is it went with the glass and ceramics content when you split that off, and since the missing element breaks nothing it’s just a compromise to reduce raw footprint if someone chooses not to install Glass & Ceramics.

Flagging it in case it’s news. If you want it back, fix in argmod_materials.json since both those materials are injected: add SALT_GLAZE as an injected material and point at ARGMOD_SALT_GLAZE, or drop the two reaction_products entries to stop pointing at a ghost.

Worth saying: If you like how this port works, I’d be glad to port the remaining offshoots. If the footprint can be reduced enough you might even be able to run the whole suite with less impact than the core mod in raw format. That’s my goal anyway.

3. Seven acids have a malformed [STATE_COLOR:CLEAR]

AMMONIA_SOLUTION, SODIUM_HYDROXIDE, SULFURIC_ACID, NITRIC_ACID, HYDROCHLORIC_ACID, HYDROFLUORIC_ACID and AQUA_REGIA are all missing the state selector, which DF shouldn’t be able to parse. I’d expect that means your intended ivory, cream and vermilion never took effect and those materials have been falling back to the template’s CLEAR, but I didn’t verify that in game, so treat it as likely rather than established. The port carries CLEAR either way, matching the token as written.

Fix in argmod_materials.json, and it’s easier than it was in raws, because JSON has no malformed-token failure mode, so there’s no selector to forget:

"color": "IVORY"

I want to be straight about all four, including the two where my first instinct was wrong.

Cut-and-replace: don’t change this yet

My initial idea was that you cut vanilla reactions without redefining them and inject your replacements instead, freeing five materials and moving 21 reactions into the editable half. Having thought about it properly, I don’t think you should, at least not now.

Why you probably cut-and-replace in the first place. I imagine it’s to inherit the vanilla reaction’s behaviour so it lands in the right workshop and carries whatever internal state makes it act like a real reaction. It’s worth knowing the engine addresses this differently: an injected reaction clones a structurally similar vanilla reaction as a template (matched on building type, subtype, fuel flag, reagent count) and then overwrites its identity and contents. So it gets genuine vanilla internals rather than a synthesized shell. Nothing is hardcoded. Every injected reaction is scored against the available vanilla ones and takes the best match. STEEL_MAKING2 currently scores BRASS_MAKING highest; the ore reductions land on BITUMINOUS_COAL_TO_COKE. Change a reaction’s shape and it may well pick a different template next load.

But I haven’t tested that specific migration. I don’t know first-hand that cutting STEEL_MAKING and injecting a replacement cloned from BRONZE_MAKING gives identical results. I can’t see why it wouldn’t. This game does unintuitive things that break everything in the most infuriating ways, so best to approach with caution.

And the load-order problem is serious. If you cut STEEL_MAKING and your replacement becomes ARGMOD_STEEL_MAKING, every other mod that references STEEL_MAKING stops resolving. That’s not a footnote. It’s your mod silently breaking in other people’s load orders, and then you’re stuck writing compatibility patches for all of them. That risk alone outweighs five raw entries.

There’s also a template-supply issue. The engine builds injected reactions by grading and cloning vanilla ones. If you cut many smelter reactions and replace them all with injected versions, you can starve the engine of clone sources. If you ever do go down this road, leave a healthy set of vanilla reactions of each shape intact.

The real fix is on my end. The engine only adds; it can’t edit. When it can, by redefining an existing reaction in place rather than adding a new one, this problem disappears. You’d keep STEEL_MAKING under its own id, other mods keep resolving, and the definition still lives in JSON. That’s now high on my priority list precisely because of this. Until then, cut-and-replace is still the right call and I’d leave it alone.

MATERIAL_REACTION_PRODUCT versus reaction classes

This one’s a genuine tradeoff.

A REACTION_CLASS on a mineral is just a string tag. It names no material, so it creates no dependency, and an injected reaction can filter on it freely.

A MATERIAL_REACTION_PRODUCT on a mineral names a specific material. That is a hard reference, and it forces the target to stay in raws.

Your sulfide chain uses both patterns for the same job. The per-metal reactions filter on REACTION_CLASS:NICKEL_SULFIDE and name their product directly. Those are fully injected and cost nothing. The generic _ANY_SULFIDE_ORE variants derive their product through MATERIAL_REACTION_PRODUCT, and that single choice is what pins all 11 oxides and sulfates into raws.

Dropping those lines and relying on the per-metal reactions would free all 11, but at the cost of the generic reaction, which is genuinely more elegant.

There may be a way for the engine to handle generic-reagent reactions like this without the raw-side dependency. It’s not solved, and I don’t want to promise a shape for it, but it’s on the list. If the generic reactions matter to you, leaving this as-is for now will work fine.

Graphics

Eleven materials stay in raws purely so their bar tiles resolve. Graphics bindings are parsed from raws and name materials directly, and there’s no way around that until the engine can inject graphics. Possible, but a new subsystem rather than a tweak. No action on your side.

CHROMIUM, COBALT, MANGANESE

These come from Elemental Metals Library, which you cut and redefine. Freeing them means dealing with the library itself, either porting it for injection or absorbing the relevant definitions into the ArgMOD port’s JSON.

That’s a bigger conversation than this port, and it has the same load-order hazard as above: the library exists to be depended on via raws, so converting it would break anything else that depends on it.

If you were starting from scratch

  • Anything that spawns in the ground stays raw. Not negotiable.
  • Tag minerals with reaction classes rather than pointing them at materials.
  • Add new content by injection; keep cuts and edits in raws, until the engine can edit, at which point most of this gets simpler.
  • Keep custom graphics for materials that were always going to be raw anyway.

The split will feel like something to work around at first, because ArgMOD wasn’t written with it in mind and the line falls in some odd places as a result. Most of the awkwardness can be designed away, but not all of it, and some of it is waiting on me rather than you and will improve as I continue to develop RM.
